How we publish

Editorial policy

The standards behind our practical automation guides, including corrections, commercial relationships, and the use of AI in our own work.

Our editorial standard

We aim to publish useful, specific, and honest guidance. Articles explain a workflow, its boundaries, and the checks that make it safer. We avoid claims that automation is effortless, universal, or a replacement for people.

Research and testing

We prefer first-hand workflow reasoning, official product documentation, and clearly described examples. Tool interfaces and policies change, so readers should verify current behavior before putting advice into production. We do not present a personal experiment as a universal benchmark.

AI-assisted writing

AI tools may help us outline, compare wording, or find gaps in a draft. A human editor is responsible for the final article, examples, recommendations, and safety boundaries. We do not publish unreviewed model output as editorial work.

Commercial relationships

We may earn revenue from advertising or clearly disclosed affiliate links in the future. Commercial relationships do not guarantee coverage or positive treatment. If a relationship could affect an article, we will disclose it near the relevant recommendation and update the page if the relationship changes.

Corrections and updates

If you find a factual error, broken link, unsafe instruction, or outdated product detail, contact us with the page URL and the correction. We will review credible reports and make material corrections visible. Article dates reflect the latest substantial editorial update shown on the page.

Our test: Can a thoughtful reader understand what the workflow does, where it can fail, and who remains accountable?
